# Career and Research Positioning

> [!goal]
> Turn broad graph competence into a clear professional identity supported by public evidence, domain advantage and a credible research trajectory.

## 1. Choose a T-shaped identity

Your horizontal breadth is the complete graph field. Choose one primary vertical and one secondary vertical.

Possible primary identities:

- ontology/semantic knowledge engineer;
- knowledge graph platform engineer;
- graph data scientist/network scientist;
- graph ML researcher;
- KG + LLM/GraphRAG engineer;
- ontology-grounded agent researcher;
- scientific/legal/enterprise KG specialist.

A strong positioning for your background could be:

> **Knowledge Graph and Agentic AI engineer/researcher specializing in ontology-grounded, evidence-traceable agents and graph learning over enterprise data.**

Prove breadth through the vault/projects, but communicate one coherent problem you solve.

## 2. Skill evidence matrix

| Capability | Evidence employers/reviewers trust |
|---|---|
| Ontology | OWL/SHACL ontology, CQs, reasoner tests, governance |
| Graph DB | profiled Cypher/SPARQL, query plans, data model and operations |
| KG construction | mappings, provenance, ER metrics, quality pipeline |
| Analytics | stability/null models and decision impact |
| Graph ML | leakage-safe baselines, ablations, reproducible results |
| GraphRAG | evidence-labeled benchmark, retrieval and faithfulness metrics |
| Agents | typed tools, authorization, traces, red-team results |
| Research | reproduction, literature map, controlled extension, writing |

Certificates are supporting signals; artifacts and defensible decisions are primary.

## 3. Portfolio architecture

Create one landing page with:

- 2-sentence specialization;
- architecture diagram of your capstone;
- 4 flagship projects;
- one research reproduction;
- writing/talks;
- skill matrix;
- contact and repository links.

For each flagship project show problem, graph necessity, design alternatives, quantitative result, failure and reproducibility.

## 4. Flagship set

Recommended four:

1. tested ontology + governed KG;
2. evidence-first extraction/entity-resolution pipeline;
3. graph ML experiment with realistic splits;
4. ontology-grounded GraphRAG/agent with safety evaluation.

The capstone can integrate all four, but keep individual modules understandable.

## 5. Resume language

Prefer evidence:

- "Designed an OWL/SHACL ontology with 35 competency questions and CI regression tests";
- "Built a replayable KG pipeline across PostgreSQL, documents and Neo4j with assertion-level provenance";
- "Reduced false entity merges by X under a consequence-weighted evaluation";
- "Benchmarked vector, structured and graph retrieval across 200 evidence-labeled questions; identified query classes where graph retrieval improved recall";
- "Implemented typed, least-privilege graph tools and adversarial tests for an agentic assistant."

Avoid "worked on knowledge graphs" or tool lists without outcomes.

## 6. Interview preparation

Be ready to whiteboard:

- ontology versus schema versus taxonomy;
- RDF/OWL versus property graph tradeoffs;
- open-world semantics and validation;
- event/n-ary/provenance modeling;
- entity resolution pipeline;
- Cypher/SPARQL query and plan;
- centrality/community assumptions;
- KGE/GNN split leakage;
- GraphRAG versus vector RAG;
- safe NL-to-query and agent architecture;
- production incident and recovery.

For every answer include one tradeoff and one failure mode.

## 7. Research community map

Track work from venues/communities including:

- ISWC, ESWC, Knowledge Graph Conference and SEMANTiCS for semantic Web/KGs;
- The Web Conference, SIGIR, KDD, WSDM and CIKM for retrieval/data mining;
- SIGMOD, VLDB and ICDE for graph data systems;
- NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, KDD and AAAI/IJCAI for graph ML/AI;
- ACL, EMNLP and NAACL for KG/LLM extraction, QA and agents;
- graph/network-science communities such as NetSci;
- domain venues for legal, biomedical, scientific or industrial applications.

Follow proceedings and workshops, not only social-media summaries.

## 8. Publication ladder

1. rigorous technical blog/reproduction report;
2. open-source dataset/ontology/tool artifact;
3. workshop or industry track submission;
4. benchmark/system/demo paper;
5. full research paper with controlled novelty;
6. sustained research agenda or graduate research proposal.

A well-documented negative result can build credibility and sharpen the next study.

## 9. Domain advantage

Your existing experience with enterprise data, agentic pipelines, Snowflake/PostgreSQL, legal KGs and dealer coaching can provide rare research assets:

- real schema/identity/provenance complexity;
- questions requiring structured and textual evidence;
- temporal policy/program logic;
- operational agent evaluation;
- production constraints absent in toy benchmarks.

Use only data you are authorized to use. Recreate shareable synthetic/open analogues for public work.

## 10. Open-source strategy

Contribute small, high-quality changes:

- documentation/example correction;
- benchmark adapter;
- dataset loader;
- SHACL shapes or ontology module;
- reproducibility fix;
- evaluation script;
- issue with minimal reproducer.

Consistent contributions demonstrate engineering judgment better than an abandoned large repository.

## 11. Teaching as mastery

Create a sequence of concise technical notes/talks:

1. KG versus ontology versus graph DB;
2. open-world reasoning versus validation;
3. RDF and LPG modeling of one domain;
4. entity resolution failures;
5. graph algorithm assumptions;
6. leakage in KGE/GNN evaluation;
7. when GraphRAG helps;
8. ontology-grounded agent safety.

Teaching reveals conceptual gaps and creates discoverable evidence.

## 12. Two-year trajectory

### Months 0-6

Foundations, ontology, graph systems, KG pipeline and analytics. Release Projects 1-5.

### Months 7-12

Graph ML, GraphRAG and agent systems. Reproduce one paper. Release Projects 6-10.

### Months 13-18

Select one frontier; run controlled studies; submit a workshop/industry/demo or strong public report.

### Months 19-24

Deepen one thesis-sized question, extend across domains, collaborate with researchers/domain experts and target a full paper or production research impact.

## 13. Quarterly scorecard

| Area | Evidence this quarter |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | chapters/standards mastered |
| Engineering | systems shipped and operated |
| Evaluation | benchmarks, baselines and failures |
| Research | papers read/reproduced/extended |
| Communication | article, talk, documentation |
| Community | contribution, review or collaboration |
| Domain impact | decision or workflow improved |

## 14. Final positioning test

A reviewer should be able to answer from your public work:

- What difficult graph problems can this person solve?
- Do they understand semantics and systems, not only libraries?
- Can they evaluate ML/LLM claims rigorously?
- Can they build safe production systems?
- What research question are they pursuing next?

When those answers are obvious, your learning has become professional and research capital.
