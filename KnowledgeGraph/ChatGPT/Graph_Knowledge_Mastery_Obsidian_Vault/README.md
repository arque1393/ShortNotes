# Graph Knowledge Mastery - Obsidian Vault

A chapter-wise study, engineering and research system for mastering the complete graph field: ontology and formal knowledge representation, knowledge graphs, graph databases, graph analytics, graph data science, graph machine learning, GraphRAG and ontology-grounded agents.

This edition contains **more than 60,000 words across 67 interconnected Markdown notes**, including 49 numbered chapters/reference notes, a 64-week curriculum, 44 labs, 12 advanced projects, current standards guidance, reading/video catalogs and reusable research templates.

## Start here

1. Unzip the folder.
2. In Obsidian, select **Open folder as vault**.
3. Choose `Graph_Knowledge_Mastery_Obsidian_Vault`.
4. Open [[00_Graph_Field_Mastery_Home]].
5. Record your start date in [[04_Progress_Dashboard]].
6. Follow the exact first sprint in [[35_First_30_60_90_Days]].

The vault is plain Markdown. It remains readable in any editor and does not require community plugins.

## Design principles

The book follows dependency order while keeping three tracks visible:

1. **Semantics track** - logic, identity, ontology, RDF/OWL, constraints and reasoning.
2. **Systems track** - graph models, storage, queries, indexes, transactions and distributed processing.
3. **Learning track** - analytics, embeddings, GNNs, graph transformers and LLM/KG systems.

The chapters repeatedly separate concepts that are often incorrectly merged:

- ontology axioms versus data-validation constraints;
- open-world inference versus closed application checks;
- RDF graphs versus labeled property graphs;
- asserted, inferred, computed, predicted and generated claims;
- descriptive graph patterns versus causal conclusions;
- link-prediction plausibility versus logical entailment;
- GraphRAG marketing labels versus measurable retrieval architectures.

## How to study

Do not mark a chapter complete because it was read. Produce three forms of evidence:

1. **explanation** - teach the concept in your own words and state its failure conditions;
2. **implementation** - build the query, algorithm, ontology or model without copying a full tutorial;
3. **evaluation** - test correctness, leakage, performance, uncertainty and a simpler baseline.

Use [[01_How_to_Study_This_Book]] for the full learning loop, [[36_Labs_and_Exercises]] for implementation work and [[04_Progress_Dashboard]] for tracking.

## Research and source policy

- Formal claims prioritize standards, peer-reviewed papers, official documentation and authoritative books.
- Vendor courses and conference recordings are labeled as implementation guidance or case studies rather than independent evidence.
- Recent resources are organized in [[43_Courses_and_Video_Lectures]] and [[44_Papers_by_Topic]].
- Every production design should re-check versions, licenses and specification status on its decision date.
- The paper and experiment templates require assumptions, baselines, leakage checks, failure analysis and reproducibility details.
